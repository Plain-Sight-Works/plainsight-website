# Focus brief — Costa Rica Treatment Center

Facts confirmed by Adrian, 2026-08-10. Everything in `src/content/work/05-crtc.md`
comes from here.

## The client

Costa Rica Treatment Center. Addiction treatment, international patients. Most
site visitors are families deciding whether to send a family member abroad for
treatment.

## The old site

- The same programs were split across three separate sections.
- The treatment approach sat several levels deep, under the physical locations.
- Trust material (founders, clinical team, accreditations, testimonials) was
  spread across the site rather than gathered anywhere.
- It had a Spanish translation, but it was broken in places.

**The design was a problem in its own right, not a secondary one.** Adrian:
"the design was also shit. It didn't convey any warmth, it looked dated, it
looked too clinical." It had also not been maintained, and WordPress updates
had broken things over time. A big part of the value delivered here was a new
design that is warmer, more modern, and inspires more trust and familiarity.
Any draft that frames this as an arrangement problem with a visual touch-up is
wrong. The maintenance breakage does not need spelling out in the case study.

## The audit

Five automated, agent-driven sessions across the whole site before any design
work. Found a lot; the headline was that content distribution and navigation
were not intuitive. The work that came out of it was an information
architecture revamp, not a visual one.

## What we did

- Reorganized around the questions a family asks, in the order they ask them:
  what do you treat, how do you treat it, where does it happen, how do I start.
- Programs consolidated from three sections into one hub.
- Approach and locations each given their own level instead of one nested
  inside the other.
- Trust material gathered into one place.
- Kept the clinical credibility, added warmth and sense of place.
- Palette: brand blue-teal stepped back, forest green forward, so it reads as
  the Costa Rican landscape rather than a clinic.
- Photography: editorial, nature-forward, the setting as part of the treatment.
- Type: warm serif for feeling, clean sans for information.
- English and Spanish both built in full.
- Client asked for WordPress. We proposed Astro and Sanity instead.

## Art direction

Adrian wrote a photography direction brief for the client to hand to their
photographer, built from a set of visual references he pulled together. It is
in the case study as one clause in the design paragraph, because it shows art
direction skill. **Do not mention that it was unbilled.**

## `results`

**The field states the net effect, and that is not a duplicate of `solution`.**
`solution` narrates decisions one at a time. `results` says what the client
ends up with when all of them are in place. Those overlap in subject, not in
content, and three separate drafts cut the field on that confusion before
Adrian stopped it.

What belongs, in Adrian's own framing: the site looks and performs better, the
trust signals are front and center, the information is arranged in a way that
follows, the design inspires trust and warmth, and the stack is modern enough
that the client is not maintaining it.

"The site is live" is not a result. Nor is a launch date on its own. When the
site does go live, a sign-off or the client's reaction on handover can be added
to what is already there.

## Do not write

- **Review count, patient count, years in operation.** Unconfirmed. The
  "more than 70 reviews / over 600 patients / a decade of care" line in the
  original draft has no source.
- **"Two clicks from the homepage."** Never verified against the built site.
- **Per-program pricing.** Unconfirmed whether every program page publishes it.
- **"Making that case is part of what won us the project."** Our read, not
  something the client said.
- **The names of the three overlapping sections.** Too granular for a case
  study. The count is the fact worth stating.
- **Anything leaning on the broken Spanish of the old site.** Adrian: don't
  focus on it.
- **"Clean and professional."** The original draft described the old design
  this way. It was not. Adrian, on seeing it survive a rewrite: "whu... I never
  said that. It was not lol." The old design was cold, clinical and dated.
- **Any sentence asserting what a visitor feels, notices or weighs.** "Dated
  enough that a family would notice," "no patience for hunting," "most of what
  the decision comes down to" were all cut for this. State the observable fact
  about the site and stop; the reader supplies the reaction.
- **"Family" more than three or four times in the body.** It ran eleven times
  in 430 words before Adrian flagged it. It earns its place where the audience
  is defined (`challenge`) and where the trust argument turns on it
  (`approach`). Everywhere else, name the thing without the noun.
- **"Not as decoration."** The setting has always been part of the treatment,
  on the old site and in the program itself. The clause defines the photography
  against something that was never true, which is the negation rule and a
  factual error at once.
- **Crediting the old design with anything.** It had no "clinical credibility"
  worth keeping. The new site's seriousness is ours, not inherited.
- **The phrase "information architecture."** Jargon. Adrian only learned the
  term recently after twenty years of building sites, and a family reading this
  case study will not know it. Say what it means instead: how the content was
  arranged, what sits under what, the order a family reads in.
