# Focus brief — Templeton Living & Lakewood Living

Facts confirmed by Adrian, 2026-08-10. Everything in
`src/content/work/07-templeton-lakewood.md` comes from here.

This brief covers **what to say about this project**. Shape, length and
structure are the same for every case study and live in
`docs/case-study-guide.md`. Read both.

## Who hired us

**Link Brokerage, a marketing agency.** They are the client. Dimex Group is the
builder and developer of both properties, and we never worked for Dimex
directly. Any sentence saying Dimex came to us, hired us, asked us for
something or requested anything is wrong.

The job: redesign the Templeton sales brochure, and redesign both websites.

Templeton and Lakewood are sibling Passive House developments by Dimex Group on
East 1st Avenue in East Vancouver. Templeton mixes condos and townhomes.
Lakewood is all three-bedroom townhomes.

## The brief had two halves

Modernize, and **narrow**. Both properties had been for sale for a couple of
years and not every floor plan was still available. The sites had to be
refreshed and refocused on the final stage of the sales process, presenting
what was left rather than the full catalogue.

The second half is the strategic story of this project. A draft that covers
only the redesign has missed it.

## The old sites

Both existed, both were in use, and the two were very similar to each other.
Who built them is unknown and does not matter.

- Dated overall.
- The hero was clunky and cluttered.
- The first section below it was a large video thumbnail. The video was
  embedded straight onto the page, so the thumbnail was a random frame of it.
- Further down, information about Passive House, the features and the homes sat
  inside a slider, several slides deep, with nothing on the page saying there
  was more behind it.
- Templeton's site already had a floor-plan filter.

**Do not write this as a rescue.** Adrian: "we didn't really create anything
very innovative or different, we just improved the way that the information was
laid out and improved the design a lot." That is the honest scope and the case
study holds to it.

Nobody is known to have complained. No sentence may assert what a buyer, the
sales team or the agency felt, noticed, wanted or would have missed.

## The brochure

**Templeton only, delivered as a PDF. There is no Lakewood brochure.**

The design system was designed for the brochure first, then adapted for the
websites. Close rather than identical, deliberately, so the marketing materials
read as one thing across print and screen.

## What we did

- Built one design system, brochure first, then both sites on it.
- Dimex's logo and brand colours already existed and stayed. Templeton magenta,
  Lakewood teal.
- **The typeface is ours.** They asked us to pick a new one. We chose a serif to
  move the sites off the cold, technical feel of the previous font.
- Laid the information out better and improved the design. No new mechanic was
  invented.
- **The neighbourhood maps are the only asset we made.** Redrawn to simplify
  them and improve how they look.
- Everything else — renders, interior photography, the walkthrough video — was
  supplied.
- We did not write the copy. We tightened a lot of it. One clause at most, and
  only if it does not read as a claim on work that was not ours.
- Built as working prototypes, then rebuilt in Wix Studio, which the client
  asked for so they could edit the pages themselves.

## No `results` field

There is no result to state. No client reaction was recorded: the work was
accepted and that was the end of it. Both sites are live. Sales performance
belongs to Dimex and stays out entirely.

Everything else that could go in the field restates `solution`, which is the
guide's own test for cutting it. **Do not reinstate it.**

## Do not write

- **Dimex as the client.** Link Brokerage hired us.
- **A Lakewood brochure.** It does not exist.
- **"The second site took a fraction of the time."** True, but it is a fact
  about our effort rather than the client's outcome, and it invites the reader
  to price the second site lower.
- **Sanity.** We proposed it and the client chose Wix Studio. Naming the option
  we lost reads as a complaint. State what was built.
- **Anything about the Wix build being slow or painful to work in.** Same reason.
- **Sales numbers, "80% sold", or any claim about how the homes are selling.**
- **Any claim of innovation.** No novel mechanic was built. The value is in the
  layout, the design and the narrowed focus.
- **Anything asserting what a visitor felt or would have missed.** The slider
  held information several slides deep and nothing on the page announced it.
  That is the observable fact. Whether a buyer was frustrated by it is not ours
  to say.
