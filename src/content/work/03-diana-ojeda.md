challenge: "Diana Ojeda is a Gestalt coach in Toronto. Her practice lived on awarenessandflow.com, a joint site she co-founded, where her coaching was one entry in a Services dropdown alongside her co-founder's Tai Chi and Qigong classes. She was moving the coaching to the front of what she offers, and she works in three ways: Gestalt, Zen Shiatsu and Biodanza. The obvious build is three service pages, and that is the problem rather than the answer. Three pages turn one practice into a menu, and a menu asks the visitor to compare and choose before anything has been explained. The people who come to her are stretched thin and already carrying more open decisions than they want, and one more four-way comparison is the last thing to hand them. They are also wary of wellness language, so the usual copy that smooths a choice like that over was never available either."
approach: |
  We led with the state someone arrives in rather than the name of a technique, and we kept the three modalities as one offer instead of three products. That settled the shape of the homepage. Where a site like this normally puts a grid of service cards, we put a single sentence the visitor finishes: Right now, I feel ___, with four states under it to choose from. Overwhelmed. Stuck. Disconnected. Unfulfilled.

  The reason to build it that way is that the interaction does the work rather than describing it. Gestalt is not a technique you read about and then agree to. It works with what is happening in a person now rather than with an account of what happened to them before, and it starts from what they notice in themselves rather than from a label someone else hands them. So that is what the block asks for: notice how you feel, in the present tense. The four options are states a person arrives in rather than services on offer, which is exactly what keeps it from being a menu, and the visitor has done a small piece of the work before reading a line about the method.

  One felt choice is also less work than a four-way scan. Choosing a state fills the blank in the sentence and brings back one line of recognition and one way forward, so the site puts the need in front of the visitor instead of laying out everything on offer and asking them to guess which part is theirs.

  It holds only while the copy stays first person and present tense. The moment those four states become service names in a dropdown, the sentence stops being the visitor's and turns into a label pushed at them, and the block collapses into the menu it was built to avoid. That constraint governs every edit to it from here.
solution: |
  We did not open the homepage on the check-in. It opens on Diana: a photograph of her, one line naming the state her clients arrive in, and one thing to do about it, which is book a free exploration call. A band of credentials follows, twenty years of embodied practice and her accreditations from the International Coaching Federation and the Gestalt Institute of Cleveland. Three facts, stated once on the homepage and then left alone. The check-in comes next, in the slot a service grid would have taken.

  Choosing a state writes it into the sentence and opens a card under it carrying one line back and one button. Switching states swaps both. The four options are a radio group, so the whole thing runs from the keyboard, and with JavaScript off it degrades to four plain sentences a crawler can follow. Every state lands on the same Work With Me page rather than on a page of its own, because the point of it is that the doors open into one room.

  Work With Me is where we hold the three together rather than split them. It opens on one integrated practice, then draws it: a lotus with its roots showing, the coaching above the water and decades of Zen Shiatsu, breath and movement below. Four chapters follow. Each is headed by the state rather than the technique, and each explains its method in a sentence anyone can follow, so Zen Shiatsu arrives as sustained, listening pressure along the body's energy pathways, given through clothing, rather than as a word to go and look up. Where someone wants the long form on one modality, the chapter links out to the page that already carries it on the shared site rather than growing a service page here. The section closes by saying the four are not a choice to get right: they run together or on their own, and nobody has to know which door is theirs before starting.

  The palette is three hues on warm cream, ocean, clay and sage, with no fourth. Colour arrives in bands and accents rather than across whole surfaces, which keeps the page calm without letting it go pale, the usual failure in this category. Motion is gentle, and it honours a reduced-motion preference throughout. Four pages carry all of it: Home, About, Work With Me, Contact.
